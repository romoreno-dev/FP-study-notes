    PERFECT MODALS

    MUST HAVE

    Deducción lógica sobre algo que ha sucedido en el pasado

    • He didn't hear any voice. The thief must have gone.
    • The kitchen was clean. They must have gone to have lunch out.

    CAN'T HAVE

    Deducción sobre algo que no pasó en el pasado basado en las evidencias del presente.

    • He can't have got the job.
    • She can't have passed such a difficult exam.

    MIGHT/MAY/COULD HAVE

    Expresa posibilidad en el pasado

    • He didn't even know how to program. They couldn't have given him the job.
    • The little girl may have lost her keys
    • Our neighbors might have heard some noises when our car was stolen

    COULD HAVE

    Posibilidad en el pasado pero no se llevó a cabo. ahora es tarde. - She could have called the doctor early in the morning. - You could have been a good doctor, but you decided to study engineering.

    SHOULD HAVE || OUGHT TO HAVE

    Algo deseable en el pasado (opinión) pero ahora es tarde Deber u obligación incumplido - He should have taken some advice from his friend but he didn't - She should have adked you before borrowing your pen - Paul ought to have waited until the lights were green before he crossed the street, but he didn't wait.

    SHOULDN'T HAVE

    Arrepentimiento sobre hecho pasado. Tuvo lugar pero no era deseable.

    • They shouldn't have gone out late last night, but they did.
    • She shouldn't have taken the matter too seriously.

    NEEDN'T HAVE

    Algo se hizo pero no era necesario. - When the party was over, I realized that you needn't have cooked so much food as most of it was untouched.

    WOULD HAVE

    En el Tercer condicional (Condición irreal del pasado que habría determinado otra del pasado) - If my parents had had more money, I would have gone to university. Y en el mixto 2+3 (Condición irreal del presente que determina una del pasado) - If I were rich, I would have bought that expensive car.

    2º Condicional

    (Condición irreal del presente que determina una del presente) - If I were rich, I would spend my life travelling Y en el mixto 3 + 2 (Condición irreal del pasado que determina una del presente) - If I had studied harder, I would find a good job. (now)

    WISH

    • Expresa deseos quejas y lamentos

    En el presente: WISH + PRON + PAST SIMPLE

    • It's raining! I wish I had an umbrella
    • I wish I lived here
    • Do you ever wish you could fly? Puede usarse were
    • I wish I were in Scotland
    • I wish she were here now.

    En el pasado: WISH + PRON + PAST PERFECT

    • I wish I had never born
    • I wish I had never say that
    • I wish I had known that Gary was ill
    • I wish we hadn't gone to bed so late last night
    • I wish I had seen him
    • Do you wish you'd studied science instead of language?
    • I wish I could have gone

    En el futuro: WISH + PRON + COULD / WOULD + INF

    • I wish I could relearn to drive.
    • I wish I would stop raining
    • I wish you could stay longer
    • I with it would stop raining
    • I wish somebody would answer it
    • I wish your wouldn't keep interrupting me

    No lo confundas con el: - Me gustaría: > I would like to relearn to drive.

    Si acaba en t y d --> /id/ Si es sorda: k, ch, sh, p, s, f, x, th. --> /t/ Si lo demas /d/ +

    💡 Diferencia con “used to” o “would”:

    1. Past simple: “We had breakfast together every morning.”

      • Simplemente relata lo que pasaba en el pasado, puede ser un hecho o rutina.

    2. Used to: “We used to have breakfast together every morning.”

      • Enfatiza que ya no lo hacemos, hábito pasado.

    3. Would: “We would have breakfast together every morning.”

      • También indica hábito pasado, más literario o narrativo.

    En resumen:

    • Past simple → rutina pasada sin comentar si sigue o no.

    • Used to / would → hábito pasado que ya no ocurre (o narrativo).
