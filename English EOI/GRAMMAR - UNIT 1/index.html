    GRAMMAR UNIT 1

    Question formation

    There are two types of questions:

    Yes-No Question

    • Se responden con patrón de sí-no
    • Siguen la estructura: Auxiliary Verb / Modal Verb + Sujeto + Main Verb ?
    • Se responden con short answers.
    Question Short Answer Long Answer
    Are you studying English? Yes, I’m | No, I’m not Yes, I am studying English | No, I am not studying English
    Is she happy? Yes, she’s | No, she isn’t Yes, she is happy | No, she is not happy
    Do you like pizza? Yes, I do | No, I don’t Yes, I like pizza | No, I don’t like pizza
    Did he call you? Yes, he did | No, he didn’t Yes, he called me | No, he didn’t call me
    Will they come? Yes, they will | No, they won’t Yes, they will come | No, they will not come
    Can she swim? Yes, she can | No, she can’t Yes, she can swim | No, she can’t swim
    Should they study more? Yes, they should | No, they shouldn’t Yes, they should study more | No, they shouldn’t study more
    Might he come tomorrow? Yes, he might | No, he might not Yes, he might come tomorrow | No, he might not come tomorrow

    Wh-Questions

    • Contienen una question word (When, Where, Who, Why, What, How, How much-many, How often)

    • Siguen la estructura: Question Word + Auxiliary Verb / Modal Verb + Suj + Main verb ?

    • Se pueden usar negative questions para expresar sorpresa ante algo inesperado o para verificar algo que se cree cierto. 

      Examples:
- Can't you swim? Yes, I can (Sí, puede nadar)
- Aren't you tired? Yes, I am  (Sí, estoy cansado)
- Haven't you done your homework? Yes, I have (Sí, la he hecho)
- Isn't it amazing? Yes, I it is (Sí, lo es)
- Isn't this a beautiful place? Yes, it is (Sí, lo es)

    • Si el verbo viene normalmente seguido de una preposición, la preposición va al final de la oración. - What are you thinking about? - Who does this bag belong to? A menudo solo se usa la question word + preposición - What about?

    A: I’m worried about something.  
B (larga): What are you thinking about?  
B (corta): About what?

A: I saw her yesterday, and she looked strange.  
B (larga): Who were you looking at?  
B (corta): Who at?

A: I was talking to someone on the phone.  
B (larga): Who were you talking to?  
B (corta): Who to?

A: I’ve been standing here for ages.  
B (larga): Who are you waiting for?  
B (corta): Who for?

A: I don’t really agree with him.  
B (larga): Who do you agree with?  
B (corta): Who with?

A: It depends on the weather.  
B (larga): What does it depend on?  
B (corta): What on?

    • Cuando la question word es el sujeto de la pregunta en present o past simple no se usa do/did. > En definitiva, si vamos a responder con un sujeto, no se pone.

      Example: Who wrotes Shakespeare? - William Shakespeare wrote him. ← formal y completa - William Shakespeare. ← suficiente en conversación

      Example: Who lives in that house? - Sheriff Wiggum - Sheriff Wiggum lives there

    WHO
Who called you yesterday?  
→ John did.  ← “Who” es el sujeto
Who did you call yesterday?  
→ I called John.  ← “Who” es el objeto

WHAT
What made that noise?  
→ The wind did.  ← “What” es el sujeto
What did you make yesterday?  
→ I made a cake.  ← “What” es el objeto

WHICH
Which of these books belongs to you?  
→ This one does.  ← “Which” es el sujeto
Which book did you buy?  
→ I bought this one.  ← “Which” es el objeto

WHOSE
Whose idea won the contest?  
→ Maria’s did.  ← “Whose idea” es el sujeto
Whose book did you borrow?  
→ I borrowed John’s.  ← “Whose book” es el objeto

WHERE
Where is happening the meeting?  ← Muy formal / raro  
Where did you go yesterday?  
→ I went to the park.  ← “Where” es el objeto de “go”

WHEN, WHY, HOW
When did you arrive?  
→ I arrived at 8.  ← “When” no es sujeto
Why did she leave early?  
→ She left because she was tired.  ← “Why” no es sujeto
How did you solve the problem?  
→ I solved it with patience.  ← “How” no es sujeto

    Indirect questions

    • Las indirect questions se usan para preguntar de una forma más educada
    • Siguen el orden sujeto + verbo y no se usa do-does/did en la segunda parte de la pregunta. Se puede usar if/wether si es una yes-no question.

    Auxiliary verbs & Modals

    Short answers

    • Did you like the film? Yes, I did
    • Are you a self-confident person? Yes I am

    Evitar repetición del verbo principal

    • I hate this kind of exams and so does my friend Ann

    So y Neither

    So + aux + subj - Peter loves this novel and so do I Neither/Nor + aux + subj - You didn't know Alex was coming today and neither/nor did I.

    Echo questions

    • A: This is a really interesting guy. B: Is he?
    • A: I really want to go on a date with him. B: Do you?

    Add emphasis

    • I know you don't believe me but I did not cheat in the exam.

    Questions tags

    Positive statement neg aux + subj Negative statement positive aux + subj

    • He's from Ireland, isn't he?
    • You haven't been to China before, have you?

    Comparative and superlative adjectives

    Una sílaba

    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    soft softer the softest
    cheap cheaper the cheapest
    sweet sweeter the sweetest
    thin thinner the thinnest
    big bigger the biggest
    wide wider the widest
    dry drier the driest

    Dos sílabas (acabados en -y)

    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    lucky luckier the luckiest
    pretty prettier the prettiest
    tidy tidier the tidiest

    Dos sílabas (acabados en -ed, -ing, -ful, -less)

    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    worried more worried the most worried
    boring more boring the most boring
    careful more careful the most careful
    useless more useless the most useless
    #### Dos sílabas (ambas posibilidades)
    • common
    • cruel
    • gentle
    • handsome
    • likely
    • narrow
    • pleasant
    • polite
    • simple
    • stupid
    • clever

    Tres sílabas

    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    dangeorus more dangeorus the most dangeorus
    difficult more difficult the most difficult

    Excepciones: Adjetivos de tres sílabas formados por añadir el prefijo -un (especialmente si acaba en -y).

    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    unhappy unhappier / more unhappy the unhappiest / the most unhappy
    #### Adjetivos irregulares
    Adjective Comparative Superlative
    good better the best
    bad worse the worst
    far farther/further the farthest / the furthest

    Usos del comparativo

    • Suele ir seguido con than y un pronombre o nombre.: I think that she's more intelligent than her sister.

    • Cualificados por palabras y frases como as much, a lot, far, a bit / little, slightly: You should go by train, it would be much cheaper. Could you be a bit quieter? I'm feeling a lot better. Do yoyu have one that's slightly biggest?

    • Pueden ser contrastados. Un cambio en una cualidad provoca un cambio en la otra.

      • The smaller the gift, the easier it is to send
      • The more stressed you are, the worse it is for your health.

    • Los comparativos pueden ser enlazados con and para mostrar un incremento continuo de una cualidad particular

      • The sea was getting rougher and rougher
      • Her illness was becoming worse and worse
      • He became more and more tired as the weeks went by.

    Usos del superlativo

    • También pueden ser colocados antes de nombres en la posición de atributo o después del verbo to be y otros verbos de enlace

      • The most delicious chocolate cake I' ve ever eaten
      • Annabel was the youngest
      • This restaurant is the best

    • A menudo son usados por sí solos si queda claro qué o quién es comparado. Si se quiere especificar puede ponerse un nombre o una frase comenzando por in o of

      • Annabel was the youngest child
      • Annabel was the youngest of the children
      • This restauran is the best in town

    Contrarios del comparativo y del superlativo

    • Se usa less y the least
      • This sofa is less comfortable
      • I've always been less patient thasn my sister
      • It's the least expensive way to travel
      • She was the least intelligent of the three sisters.
